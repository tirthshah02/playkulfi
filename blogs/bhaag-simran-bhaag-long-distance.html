How to play Bhaag Simran Bhaag when you're not in the same city

It's a fast tilt-and-dodge runner built for two thumbs and one screen. Here's the version that works when those two thumbs are in different area codes.

kulfi teampublished september 20, 2026

The easiest way to play Bhaag Simran Bhaag when you're not together is to get on a video call, prop your phones up, and take turns running the same level while the other one heckles — or, if your schedules don't line up, screen-record your run and send the score instead.

Bhaag Simran Bhaag is the kind of game that looks simple for the first ten seconds and then isn't: you tilt your phone to steer Simran down a lane, dodging whatever the level throws at her, while your partner does the same on the same screen. It's built to be played shoulder to shoulder, both of you leaning into your phones like that helps. Which is a problem if you're not in the same room, or the same city, or occasionally the same time zone.

The obvious fix is to just not play it long-distance and pick something else. The better fix is to stop trying to replicate the shoulder-to-shoulder version and build the version that actually works over a call.

The live version

Get on a video call first, phones propped up so you can both see each other's screens over your partner's shoulder — screen share works too if your call app supports it. Play the same level back to back rather than at the same time, so neither of you is watching your own screen and the call at once. Whoever's turn it isn't becomes live commentary. This is where the game actually gets good: watching someone panic-tilt their phone while you narrate their incoming death is funnier over a call than it has any right to be.

Keep score across a short session — five runs each, best single run wins — rather than treating it as one match. A single round decided by a lucky gap in the obstacles feels hollow. Five rounds decided by who can actually keep their thumb steady while being heckled feels like a real result.

When you can't sync up

Some nights the call isn't happening and the game still is. Play your run, screen-record it, send the clip with your score. Your partner plays theirs cold, without watching yours first — no scouting, no adjusting to a difficulty they've already seen. Whoever's higher when both clips are in gets bragging rights until the next round, which in a long-distance relationship is a surprisingly durable currency.

This async version also fixes the time-zone problem outright. Neither of you is waiting for the other to be free. You're just leaving a small, slightly competitive voice note in game form.

Why the small ritual beats the big session

You don't need to play this for an hour to get something out of it. Five runs each, live commentary or a screen-recorded clip, takes less time than deciding what to watch together. The point isn't the game itself — it's that you did a small, slightly ridiculous thing together on a day when a call would've otherwise just been status updates.

frequently asked questions

Can you play Bhaag Simran Bhaag over a video call?

Yes — prop your phones up during a call, play the same level back to back instead of at the same time, and use the downtime to heckle whoever's running. Screen sharing works too if your call app supports it.

What if we're in different time zones?

Play it async: run the level, screen-record your score, and send the clip. Your partner plays cold without watching yours first, so nobody has an advantage from seeing the run in advance.

Is Bhaag Simran Bhaag hard to learn?

No — it's a simple tilt-and-dodge runner with about ten seconds of learning curve. The difficulty comes from staying steady while your partner narrates your mistakes.

How long does a round take?

A single run takes under a minute. A full session — five runs each, best score wins — usually takes ten to fifteen minutes including trash talk.
