Long distance

17 games to play with your partner on a video call

No board, no cards, no shopping list. Just two phones, a call, and twenty minutes you'd otherwise spend scrolling separately.

Most "video call date night" lists assume you'll cook the same recipe on camera or watch a movie in sync. Those are nice for a Saturday, but they don't solve the actual problem on a Tuesday: you're both tired, you've already asked about each other's day, and the call is starting to go quiet.

These are shorter than that. Most take five to ten minutes, need nothing you don't already have, and are built to be played mid-call without either of you having to plan ahead.

Quick ones — under five minutes

  • Two Truths and a Lie, relationship edition. Each round has to be about something from your relationship, not random facts. Gets surprisingly revealing after year one.
  • Camera roll roulette. Scroll to a random photo in your camera roll and explain it. No skipping the embarrassing ones.
  • 20 Questions, but only feelings. One of you thinks of an emotion, the other guesses with yes/no questions.
  • Would You Rather, escalating. Start silly, let the questions get more personal as you go.
  • Accent challenge. Read a text message out loud in an accent you can't do. Score each other out of ten.

Games that need a little setup

  • Compatibility quiz. Each of you privately answers questions about the other, then compares. Best played with an app that keeps score, since tallying by hand kills the pace.
  • Draw it, don't say it. Take turns describing an object using only gestures on camera.
  • This or That, rapid fire. One person calls out pairs (beach or mountains, morning or night), the other answers instantly, no thinking.
  • Story building. Alternate adding one sentence to a story. Gets absurd fast, which is the point.
  • Guess the price. Pick something in the room behind you and guess what your partner paid for it.

For when you want to go deeper

  • Never Have I Ever, couples version. Skip the drinking-game prompts and ask things you're actually curious about. We've written a full list of these here.
  • The five-year check-in. Each answer where you picture yourselves in five years, then compare notes.
  • Apology and appreciation. Each says one small thing you appreciated this week and one small thing you'd do differently. Works best with a timer so it doesn't turn into a debrief.
  • Map the day. Describe your day as if it were a video game level, including the "boss fight."
  • Song for the week. Each picks a song that matched your mood this week, play thirty seconds and explain why.
  • What I noticed. Say one specific thing you noticed about the other this week that you haven't mentioned yet.
  • Rewrite the argument. Pick a small disagreement from the past month and each replay it as you wish you'd said it. Keep it light — this works best when you're both already calm.

What actually makes these work

The pattern across long-distance couples who keep this up isn't the specific game — it's that they play something most nights rather than saving it for special occasions. A five-minute game on a Tuesday does more for the relationship than a two-hour planned date once a month, mostly because it doesn't require either of you to have energy left over.
